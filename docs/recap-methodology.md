# Recap methodology, version 2

A play qualifies after 30 seconds of actual playing time within one session. Shorter sessions contribute time but do not count as qualified plays. Paused, buffering and seeked-over time contribute zero. Pause/resume preserves a session; a replay or track transition starts another.

Native players measure elapsed playing time with monotonic clocks. Approximately every ten seconds and at transitions, they persist an interval anchored to UTC and its monotonic duration. Calendar allocation uses the interval's UTC anchor plus this duration; a wall-clock correction cannot increase listened time. A crash may lose the current uncheckpointed interval.

Intervals are split at local day/month/week/year boundaries in the device IANA zone for live recaps. The app synchronizes that zone on startup, resume and periodic visible checks; existing manual overrides are replaced. Frozen snapshots retain their original zone and coverage. A qualified play belongs to the selected calendar period containing the session start. A boundary-spanning session can contribute minutes to the next period without another qualified play. Top tracks sort by qualified plays, then played milliseconds, then stable ID. Artists aggregate normalized artist labels; albums use artist plus album labels. This version does not infer canonical artists, merge remasters or interpret acoustic meaning.

Excluded sessions contribute neither time nor rankings. A toggle during a session excludes or includes the entire current session. Original sample audio is always excluded. History deletion also deletes saved snapshots and resets the coverage start, while retaining music and playlists.

Recaps show the real collection start. Daily/monthly chart buckets outside coverage are marked unobserved, separately from recorded zero listening. Saved snapshots freeze metrics, names, track IDs and a visual seed. The internal diverse recap queue retains up to 25 available, actually qualified tracks, capped at two per artist (relaxed below ten available tracks). The visible top-ten playlist instead takes the first ten ranked songs without an artist cap. Unavailable or removed entries keep their positions in the ranking but are excluded from playback and manual playlist saving; the resulting playlist may be shorter than ten. Saving creates an ordinary editable playlist with a period-specific name and never happens automatically.

Internal snapshots preserve playback references privately. The user-facing export is PNG: Story at 2160×3840 (9:16), or Feed at 2160×2700 (4:5). The preview and download use the same rendered image. Cards show display metadata, calendar coverage dates, rounded minutes (or <1 for under one minute), counts and period-specific listening. They exclude source paths, exact timestamps, private playlist names and excluded-session data. Dates outside recorded history are marked separately; completed periods display their last included date rather than their exclusive end date.

Snapshots retain immutable metrics. If session exclusions change after a snapshot was saved, sharing that snapshot is blocked by its internal privacy revision. Generate a new recap to share the corrected history. The revision is not exported.

Desktop checkpoints cap the monotonic interval by rendered source-position progress. Seek rebases that position. This prevents system suspension or an audio stall from adding the whole elapsed timer gap to listening time. Native interruption behavior still requires device verification.
The browser adapter similarly bounds checkpoints by the audio element position. It checkpoints before programmatic seeks and rebases when playback resumes.


Your Recap / Tu resumen offers calendar weeks (Monday through Sunday), months and years. Yearly is the first default; only the timeframe preference persists, with current period selected on restart. There is no historical date selector or arrow navigation. Live views follow rollover and device-zone changes automatically. Listening intervals are clipped to recorded coverage and now; incomplete periods never imply a full observation window.

Calendar arithmetic advances local dates, not fixed 24-hour increments. Ambiguous midnight uses the earliest occurrence; missing midnight moves to the first valid instant. Skipped dates have zero-duration buckets and do not count as active/observed days. Invalid or future-period requests fail. Existing yearly snapshots retain their original storage and metrics through an in-memory compatibility projection; new snapshots freeze schema-two period metadata and buckets. No listening-history rewrite is needed.

Weekly presentation uses the ISO week number and ISO week-year (Monday-based), localized as Week / Semana. Live Weekly/Monthly/Yearly views always follow the current period and update without creating snapshots. The Save snapshot action is no longer exposed; previously stored snapshots remain readable. Actual coverage dates remain visible and are not replaced by inferred or extrapolated listening.

Artist and album top-three arrays are computed from all qualified tracks before display truncation, ordered by descending qualified plays and normalized name (then artist for tied album names). Winner fields remain for backward compatibility. Snapshots without the arrays display only their recorded winner; runners-up are not reconstructed from a truncated song list. Cards and story scenes show up to three recorded ranks without inventing missing entries.

Song and album rankings show square cover artwork with decreasing rank sizes. Album ranks retain an optional private representative track ID chosen before song-list truncation; React can load its artwork separately. That ID is excluded from display exports. Older snapshots use a matching recorded song or deterministic fallback cover. Artists and albums have separate story steps (seven steps total).
